## Core Principle: Business Outcomes, Not Technical Jargon

90% of respondents believe people use jargon to cover up that they don't know what they're talking about. 60% of B2B buyers say clear messaging influences their decision more than brand reputation.

The target reader is a non-technical SMB owner (10–100 employees) who runs their business on spreadsheets, QuickBooks, and gut instinct. They do not know or care what ETL, dbt, or a data warehouse is. They care about saving time, making better decisions, and growing revenue.

## Jargon-to-Outcome Translation Table

Always translate technical language to business language:

| Never say | Say instead |
|-----------|------------|
| "We build ETL pipelines" | "We automatically pull your data from everywhere into one place" |
| "We use dbt for data transformation" | "We clean and organize your data so it's always accurate" |
| "Cloud data warehouse" | "A single source of truth for all your business data" |
| "BI dashboards" | "Live dashboards that show your business health at a glance" |
| "API integrations" | "We connect all your tools so data flows automatically" |
| "Data governance" | "We make sure your numbers are always accurate and trustworthy" |
| "Modern data stack" | "The same technology used by billion-dollar companies — sized for your business" |
| "Data modeling" | "We organize your data so it answers your real business questions" |
| "Automated reporting" | "Reports that build themselves — no copy-pasting required" |
| "Data pipeline orchestration" | "Your data flows from every tool into one place, automatically, every day" |

## The "So What?" Ladder

Start with the technical feature, keep asking "so what?" until you reach a business outcome:

```
"We build automated data pipelines"
  → so what? → "Your reports update themselves"
  → so what? → "You stop spending 15 hours/week on manual reporting"
  → so what? → "Your team focuses on selling and serving customers"
  → so what? → "You grow faster and your weekends stop being about spreadsheets"
```

That last line is headline material. Apply this to every feature claim.

## Copywriting Frameworks

### PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solution)

Highest-performing framework for pain-driven copy. Use for individual sections.

- **Problem:** State the pain in the reader's own words
- **Agitate:** Amplify with consequences, costs, emotional weight
- **Solution:** Present Rapid Insights as the relief

**Example:**
> **P:** "You're spending 15 hours a week building reports no one trusts."
> **A:** "That's 780 hours a year. Entire months of your best people's time. And the worst part? Half those reports have errors no one catches until it costs you."
> **S:** "What if your reports updated themselves, were always accurate, and took zero hours to produce?"

### PASTOR (Full Page Framework)

Ideal for the complete page arc:

- **P**roblem — Identify the pain
- **A**mplify — Show the cost of inaction
- **S**olution — Present your approach
- **T**ransformation — Show the before/after
- **O**ffer — Explain what they get (the discovery call)
- **R**esponse — Tell them exactly what to do next

## Emotional Triggers for SMB Owners (Ranked by Potency)

1. **Fear of wasting time** (their scarcest resource):
   "Every hour wrestling with spreadsheets is an hour you're not growing"

2. **Fear of bad decisions from bad data**:
   "When your data is wrong, your decisions are wrong"

3. **Competitive anxiety**:
   "Your competitors are already making data-driven decisions"

4. **Relief and peace of mind**:
   "Know exactly where your business stands — any time, from your phone"

5. **Growth aspiration**:
   "The data setup that got you to $2M won't get you to $10M"

## Tone and Reading Level

**Target: 5th–7th grade reading level.** Simpler copy converts at 11.1% vs 5.3% for college-level writing.

**Tone:** "Knowledgeable friend" — confident but not arrogant, direct but warm, professional but jargon-free.

**Rules:**
- Short sentences. Active voice. Concrete words.
- Use "you" more than "we" — apply the We/You test on every section
- Specific numbers over vague claims ("$47K" not "significant savings", "6 weeks" not "quickly")
- Precise numbers signal real data ("52.7%" outperforms "about 50%")

## CTA Copy and Placement

### Button Copy (Ranked)

1. "Get Your Free Data Health Check" — frames the call as receiving a deliverable
2. "Book Your Free Strategy Session" — clear and specific
3. "See How Much Time You're Wasting" — curiosity + pain-driven
4. "Show Me What's Possible" — exploratory, low pressure

**Never use:** "Submit," "Contact Us," "Learn More," "Get in Touch"

### Placement Rules

Repeat the same CTA 4–5 times across the page:
- Hero section
- After problem agitation section
- After testimonials (+25% lift)
- After case studies
- Page bottom (+304% lift)

Use a sticky CTA bar after scrolling past hero (+27% conversion). On mobile, use a sticky footer bar.

### Microcopy Below Every CTA

"Free 30-minute call. We'll identify your 3 biggest data bottlenecks and what fixing them could save you — whether we work together or not. No sales pitch."

### Social Proof Below CTA (+68% lift)

Place a short testimonial about the call experience:
> "Best 30 minutes I've spent on my business all year." — Sarah, Agency Owner

### Transitional CTA for Visitors Not Ready to Call

Offer a lower-commitment lead magnet: "Data Health Scorecard" or "Spreadsheet Liberation Checklist." Use as exit-intent popup to recover 10–15% of abandoning visitors.

### Multi-Step Micro-Commitment Flow (+52% conversion)

1. Click CTA button (zero friction)
2. One qualifying question: "What's your biggest data challenge?" (3–4 clickable options)
3. Pick a time on embedded Calendly (inline, never redirect off-page)
4. Enter name and email (they're already invested)
